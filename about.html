About

The Story

Why Legacy?

Every debate ends. This event exists for the part of it that doesn't — the idea that stays in the room after the speaker sits down.

01

The Event

A stage built by students, for speakers

GFS Legacy Debates is the flagship debate event of GEMS Founders School — conceived, organised and run by its students. Over the course of the event, speakers face carefully constructed motions across multiple rounds, judged by adjudicators who reward substance, structure and honesty under pressure.

The event welcomes both seasoned competitors and first-time speakers. What it asks of everyone is the same: come prepared to argue in good faith, and be prepared to be persuaded.

02

Why Legacy?

The name is a standard, not a slogan

A speech is over in minutes. A good one isn't. We named this event after the thing a truly good argument does: it survives its own delivery. It gets quoted at dinner tables, contested in corridors, and handed — slightly changed — to the next person willing to defend it.

Legacy is what an argument becomes when the speaker is no longer there to defend it.

Each edition of the event inherits something from the one before — motions revisited with new evidence, records to beat, standards to keep. The speakers change. The argument continues.

03

The Experience

What participants can expect

Expect motions that resist easy answers — drawn from politics, ethics, technology and culture. Expect preparation rooms with more whiteboard than wall, rounds where the strongest case loses to a better-argued one, and feedback that treats you as a speaker worth improving, not a score to be filed.

Beyond the rounds themselves: a hosted opening and closing ceremony, time to meet speakers from other schools, and adjudication that explains its decisions. You will leave tired, and better.

04

What We Believe

Disagreement, done properly, is a form of respect

To argue against someone seriously is to take their thinking seriously. That conviction shapes everything about how this event is run — how motions are set, how rounds are judged, and how winners and losers are expected to treat one another.

  • Steel, not straw Answer the strongest version of the other side's case — anything less is theatre, not debate.
  • Clarity over volume A quiet argument that holds is worth more than a loud one that doesn't.
  • Changing your mind is winning The person persuaded by better evidence didn't lose the round. They got the point of it.
  • The floor belongs to everyone First-time speakers are given the same stage, the same respect, and the same honest adjudication.

Your argument lasts minutes.
Your impact can last far longer.
